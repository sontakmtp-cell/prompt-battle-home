## Proposal: resolving the check 8 / check 14 conflict (measured, uncommitted)
Full write-up: `reports/proposal-check14.md`; patch at `reports/proposal-check14.patch`.

**Root cause is a four-part lock, not one bad number:**
1. Check 14 needs `stander/charger < 0.80`. `r` is the *relative* speed, so it is
   the same for both sides → the ratio is exactly 100% for ANY `IMPACT_BASE`.
   The asymmetry is structurally absent, so a per-side term is required.
2. But a per-side term folded into `impactMul` pollutes check 8, because check 8
   derives `r` by **inverting** the reported multiplier. Measured: median r
   1.000 → 0.500.
3. Lowering `IMPACT_BASE` to buy the asymmetry breaks **check 7** (the blocking
   RPS invariant). Check 14 needs `IMPACT_BASE < 800`; check 7 needs
   `IMPACT_BASE >= 765` (margin is only **2**: 14 vs 12). Measured: check 7 fails
   at `IMPACT_BASE <= 750`.
4. `RPS_ADVANTAGE` cannot rescue check 7, because it is jointly pinned with
   `HAMMER_DAMAGE` by the "Hammer→Scissor perfect = exactly 48" criterion
   (24 × 2000 × 1e6/1e9 = 48).

**Also a real measurement bug:** check 8 was taking the median r over ALL hits in
a round robin, but can_bang.md check 18 defines the scenario as "a balanced bot
charging at full speed into a stationary bot". Fixed to that scenario, with the
round-robin median kept as an info line.

**Fix (recommended):** put the asymmetry in its own factor and leave
`IMPACT_BASE` alone. `damage = floor(base × RPS × impact(relative) × orient ×
commit(own) / 1e12)` with `commit = 675 + floor(325 × min(ownR,1000)/1000)`.
New ruleset constants `IMPACT_COMMIT_BASE`/`IMPACT_COMMIT_RANGE`, new `commitMul`
on the hit event, own closing speed exposed from the collision step.

**Result at 675/325:** check 14 100.0% → **75.7% PASS**; checks 7, 8, 12 all still
PASS; 97/97 tests; 10 of 12 pass/fail checks now pass (was 9). New digest
`3bebc8f47d6146917bab1032a82d60d50a7ff97465c7511fecce1b47a7219832`.
Sweep: 750 → 82.2% (fail), 700 → 78.8% (1.2 pts headroom), 675 → 75.7% (chosen),
650 → 72.9% but flips a fairness test.

**Caveat found:** `determinism.test.ts`'s "mirrored bots must score identically"
is fragile — it passes at 675 but breaks at 650 (asymmetry at tick 272, 750 vs
829). Not caused by the commitment factor (that is provably symmetric); it is the
pre-existing order-dependent tie-break in the separation step. That test asserts a
property the engine does not structurally guarantee and should be reformulated.

**Still failing:** checks 10 (orientation spans only 17.6% of damage) and 16
(contact density, not orientation). Both need a decision on the intended dynamic
range of the orientation multiplier.

## Checks 10 and 16 diagnosed (analysis only, no code change)
Write-up: `reports/proposal-checks-10-16.md`.

**Tried the obvious fix for check 10 and it is refuted.** Swept `ORIENT_BASE`
850 → 765 (span 17.6% → 30.7%, the ceiling is pinned at 1000 by the 48
criterion). Check 10 reads 50/50 at **every** value — zero response — while check
7's blocking margin erodes from 2 to 1. So the orientation multiplier's
*strength* is not the lever.

**Root cause, from the histogram** (`tools/check10-orientation-histogram.mjs`):
- facing: **42.0%** of hits at the 850 floor, 3.4% at the ceiling, mean 932.8
- blind:  **45.7%** of hits at the 850 floor, 2.9% at the ceiling, mean 929.7

Between 42% and 46% of ALL hits take the maximum orientation penalty, and only
~3% reach the ceiling. The cause is the **bipartite lattice** — the same fact
check 11 records. Every edge neighbour of an `up` tile is a `down` tile, so at any
contact roughly half the attacker's tiles present the wrong face, and rotating the
body turns good faces away as fast as it turns bad ones in. No brain command
controls which face a tile presents at a contact, so the dominant term is fixed
by geometry. Check 10 needs a mechanic decision (restate the criterion / compute
orientation from the tile normal / add a face-controlling rotate), not a constant.

**Check 16 is a different problem wearing the same label:** it is contact density,
not orientation. The wide wing's orientation is *better* (945.2 vs 934.1); it
loses because it lands 21% fewer hits (47,625 vs 57,642), and its bounding radius
is 5,971 vs 3,496. Check 16 reads 100% at every `ORIENT_BASE`, confirming it was
never about orientation.

**Useful pattern:** when a check does not respond to a sweep of the constant it is
supposedly about, stop tuning and measure the *distribution* of the quantity
involved. The histogram is what proved the term is dominated by a structural
effect rather than by the variable under test.

## Experiment: check 14's prescribed fix (tried, measured, reverted)
Implemented the doc's switch — price each attacker by its **own** closing speed
instead of a shared relative one, in the form `min(relative, own)` (plain "own"
is unphysical: two bots moving in parallel at equal speed have zero relative
approach but a large own speed, so a graze would price as a full-speed hit).

Measured (`balance --quick`):
- check 14: 100.0% → **88.5%** (intent achieved, still > the 80% bar)
- check 8: median r 1.000 PASS → **0.500 FAIL**
- check 10: 45/53 → **12.5/87.5** (much worse)

Net regression → **reverted**. The digest returned to exactly
`5a35d60bbc80fcdee8c32dddb696fd97dcb581931cead82dce0cce280ab18e7e`, which is a
useful way to prove a revert is behaviour-identical.

**Conclusion: checks 8 and 14 are in direct conflict.** Check 14's bar is
equivalent to requiring `IMPACT_BASE < 800` (a stander floors at `IMPACT_BASE`,
a charger reaches 1000, so the ratio floor is `IMPACT_BASE/1000`), and any
mechanism that drives a stander's `r` to 0 also drags the population median `r`
below check 8's 0.80 floor. `can_bang.md`'s quoted constants cannot satisfy both.
Do not re-attempt this without first re-deriving the thresholds from a chosen
dynamic range.
